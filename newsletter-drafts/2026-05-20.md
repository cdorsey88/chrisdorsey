## OpenAI Just Built a $4B Deployment Machine

On Monday, OpenAI launched the OpenAI Deployment Company — $4 billion in backing, 19 partner firms (Goldman Sachs, SoftBank, Bain Capital, TPG, Emergence Capital), and an acquisition of Tomoro, which drops 150 engineers into the new entity whose only job is getting AI running inside enterprises. They're calling these people "Forward Deployed Engineers." That's Palantir's playbook, down to the vocabulary: embed experts, find leverage, expand. This isn't a SaaS product. OpenAI is going direct to your customers with consultants. If you sell enterprise AI today, your competitive set just got significantly more complicated. If you buy it, you now have a very well-funded vendor that will hold your hand through deployment — and then own that relationship.

## Voice AI Hit a Billion Calls — Amazon Ring Is the Case Study

Vapi closed a $50M Series B on May 12. The number that matters: 1 billion calls processed through their platform. The customer story that matters more: Amazon Ring evaluated more than 40 AI voice vendors before last holiday season and chose Vapi to handle 100% of its inbound calls. Not a pilot. All of them. Voice AI isn't a wedge anymore — it's taking over the phones at scale, and the enterprises that moved early are already out of beta. If you haven't pressure-tested voice AI for your inbound or SDR function this year, you're probably a year behind.

## Akamai Just Made GEO Infrastructure

When a CDN giant builds a product for a category, you know that category has moved from "early adopter curiosity" to "table stakes in the next 18 months." On May 19, Akamai launched AI Brand Presence — a platform specifically designed to help brands appear when AI agents are doing the searching, not humans. Akamai built its business on making sure your website loaded in under 200ms. Now it's building the same infrastructure layer for AI visibility. SEO took a decade to professionalize. GEO is moving faster. If your marketing team isn't actively working on LLM-answer presence, this is a signal worth forwarding.

## Your CRM Is Becoming an API

At TrailblazerDX in April, Salesforce dropped what may be its most significant product shift in 27 years: Headless 360. The entire platform — data, workflows, business logic — is now exposed as an API and MCP toolkit that AI agents can operate directly, no browser required. Sixty-plus new MCP tools. Agentforce Vibes IDE bundled free for developers. That means AI agents can execute CRM tasks, pull pipeline data, and trigger workflows without a human navigating the UI. For sellers, the immediate implication is that "CRM hygiene" is no longer a training problem — it becomes an agent problem. Your stack just got more powerful, but the implementations that work will require someone who understands both the AI layer and the Salesforce layer.
