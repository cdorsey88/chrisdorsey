## Anthropic's Mythos Can Break Into Everything

Anthropic dropped something significant last month and it keeps getting more relevant: Claude Mythos Preview — their unreleased frontier model — is autonomously finding zero-day vulnerabilities across every major OS and browser, faster than human red teams. In limited access via Project Glasswing (partners include Amazon, Apple, Cisco, Microsoft, and CrowdStrike), Mythos exploited a 17-year-old remote code execution flaw in FreeBSD — fully autonomously. The company didn't train it to do this. It's a downstream consequence of better general reasoning. That's the buried headline: AI that gets generally smarter is also getting generally more dangerous. The enterprise security posture conversation is about to accelerate, and every CISO you sell to is about to feel it.

## Sierra Raises $950M. Bret Taylor Is Playing a Long Game.

Bret Taylor's AI customer service company Sierra closed $950M this week at a $15.8B valuation, led by Tiger Global and GV. They're at $150M+ ARR and already serving 40%+ of the Fortune 50. The interesting move isn't the number — it's the positioning shift. Sierra is moving beyond customer support and positioning as the layer that handles *all* customer interactions at scale. If that plays out, the traditional call center software stack (NICE, Genesys, Verint) faces a credible existential threat. For enterprise sellers: Sierra is either your next competitor or your next partner. Worth forming a view now.

## Your Google Rankings Don't Mean What They Used To

GEO research firm Brandlight put a number on something everyone's been sensing: the overlap between top Google results and AI-cited sources has collapsed from 70% to under 20%. One in three US consumers now uses AI search. For 34% of B2B marketers, AI platforms are where qualified prospects *first* encounter their company. The SEO playbook most teams are running optimizes for a game that's shifting fast. The companies figuring out the answer layer now are building a durable moat. The ones sleeping on it are building invisible.

## The GTM AI Gap Is a People Problem, Not a Model Problem

Stanford's 2026 AI Index Report put two numbers on the same page that tell the whole story: agentic AI delivers a 71% median productivity gain where deployed — but only 20% of enterprises are actually using it. And 95% of companies that deploy generative AI report little to no ROI. The gap isn't the technology. Stanford found that 77% of AI implementation failures trace back to change management and data architecture, not the model itself. If you're selling AI-adjacent products right now, this is your conversation opener.

## Take Worth Reading: Strip What Doesn't Compound

Maja Voje published ["3 Big Ideas for the AI-First GTM Era"](https://knowledge.gtmstrategist.com/p/3-big-ideas-for-the-ai-first-gtm) this week, and one line stuck: *the 2026 GTM playbook isn't about doing more things faster — it's about stripping everything that doesn't compound.* The teams internalizing this first are the ones we'll be writing about in 18 months. Worth 10 minutes.
